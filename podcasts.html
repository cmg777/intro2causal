CHAPTER 1 / RANDOMIZED TRIALS

Does health insurance make people healthier?

38:56 duration
Explore the mind map ↗

People with health insurance often report better health. But did coverage make the difference? Follow the RAND experiment and Oregon lottery to see how random assignment helps separate the effect of insurance from the advantages people already have.

Listen for

  • Recognize selection bias in an ordinary comparison.
  • Distinguish an offer of insurance from receiving it.
  • Read health outcomes alongside their uncertainty.

CHAPTER 2 / REGRESSION

Is an elite college worth the extra tuition?

39:31 duration
Explore the mind map ↗

Graduates of selective colleges tend to earn more. How much comes from the college, and how much came with the student? Explore controlled comparisons, college application patterns, and simulations that reveal what regression can—and cannot—resolve.

Listen for

  • Explain what it means to hold a variable fixed.
  • Trace the direction of omitted variables bias.
  • Recognize when an extra control changes the question.

CHAPTER 3 / INSTRUMENTAL VARIABLES

What if people ignore their assigned treatment?

35:22 duration
Explore the mind map ↗

A school lottery offers a place, but not every winner attends. Start with the difference between an offer and an action, then follow school, policing, and family-size examples to understand how a random influence can help identify a treatment effect.

Listen for

  • Connect the first stage, reduced form, and IV ratio.
  • Identify whose treatment effect the estimate describes.
  • Ask whether the instrument has another path to the outcome.

CHAPTER 4 / REGRESSION DISCONTINUITY

Can a birthday reveal the effect of alcohol access?

35:11 duration
Explore the mind map ↗

Legal access to alcohol changes at a cutoff; most other features of a person change gradually. Explore drinking-age rules and selective-school admissions to see when comparisons just above and below a threshold can support a causal claim.

Listen for

  • Explain why continuity near the cutoff matters.
  • Distinguish sharp from fuzzy discontinuity designs.
  • Describe the local population and limits of an estimated jump.

CHAPTER 5 / DIFFERENCES-IN-DIFFERENCES

Did emergency lending save banks?

31:55 duration
Explore the mind map ↗

During the Great Depression, Mississippi banks faced a shared panic but different central-bank responses. Follow their changing fortunes, then examine drinking-age laws, to learn how a comparison group can help reconstruct what might have happened without a policy.

Listen for

  • Build a comparison from changes in two groups.
  • Explain the parallel-trends assumption.
  • Separate uncertainty checks from threats to the causal design.

CHAPTER 6 / THE WAGES OF SCHOOLING

Does more education cause higher earnings?

45:33 duration
Explore the mind map ↗

More schooling and higher wages often go together. Bring the course’s methods together through twins, birth timing, policy changes, and exam cutoffs—and ask how a return to learning differs from a return to a diploma.

Listen for

  • Compare the assumptions behind different research designs.
  • Distinguish an extra year of schooling from a credential.
